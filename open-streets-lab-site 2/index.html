06 / SITUATED TECHNOLOGY

THE TOOL IS SITUATED IN NEW YORK’S UNEVEN DISTRIBUTION OF PUBLIC SPACE—AND IN THE LABOR REQUIRED TO MAINTAIN IT.

DATATIMESTAFFSTORAGEFUNDINGLOCAL TRUST
WHO HAS ENOUGH CAPACITY TO APPLY?

Open Streets are public, but their creation often depends on partner organizations that can navigate applications, outreach, programming, storage, maintenance, and reimbursement.

A scoring system is also situated: the variables and weights chosen by the designer shape which places appear to “need” intervention.

THE TOOL SHOULD EXPAND COMMUNITY CAPACITY—NOT AUTOMATE AWAY COMMUNITY DECISION-MAKING.

10 / RHETORICAL ARGUMENT

ACCESS TO PUBLIC STREET SPACE SHOULD NOT DEPEND ONLY ON A COMMUNITY’S ABILITY TO NAVIGATE DATA, DESIGN, BUREAUCRACY, AND UNPAID LABOR.

A civic tool can make information and early planning more accessible.

But software cannot replace public funding, community organizing, operational support, or political negotiation.

11 / POTENTIAL CAPSTONE OUTLINE

AN INTEGRATED WEB PLATFORM WITH THREE WORKING MODULES AND ONE CLEAR USER JOURNEY.

01FIND

Use equity and context layers to identify areas for further research.

02LEARN

Understand Open Street types, precedent sites, and community responsibilities.

03BUILD

Configure a street scenario and expose access and labor trade-offs.

04DISCUSS

Use visuals to collect support, concerns, and local knowledge.

05PREPARE

Generate an early brief and checklist for official application work.

The capstone will prototype a decision-support and capacity-building process. It will not automatically select sites, predict community consent, or submit an official application.

12 / THE CHALLENGE

THE PROJECT MUST BE USEFUL WITHOUT TURNING COMPLEX PUBLIC DECISIONS INTO A FALSELY OBJECTIVE SCORE.

DATA

Public datasets may be incomplete, differently scaled, or unable to represent lived experience.

BIAS

Every index embeds decisions about what counts as need and whose needs receive weight.

RELATIONSHIPS

A credible project requires contact with real operators, schools, residents, and advocates.

CONFLICT

Delivery, disability access, parking, safety, noise, and public-space goals may collide.

TECHNICAL SCOPE

Three modules must share one data model without becoming a city-scale professional planning system.

VALIDATION

The interface must be tested for clarity and usefulness rather than judged only by visual polish.

13 / EXHIBITION COMPONENT PROPOSALS

THE WEBSITE WILL BE ACCOMPANIED BY TWO PHYSICAL WAYS OF READING AND RECONFIGURING A STREET.

PROPOSAL FOR DRAWING TYPE

A STREET IS MORE THAN A ROAD

Vertical printed street data-scape

TRAFFIC
NEIGHBORHOOD NEED
HOURS + DAYS
PUBLIC USE
LABOR + OPERATIONS

The tall drawing will unfold one street block through synchronized layers: its existing road allocation, local public-space needs, possible Open Street schedules, spatial configurations, access conflicts, and the labor required to operate it.

Rather than repeating the website, it will turn a single block into a dense vertical atlas—showing that an Open Street is simultaneously a spatial design, a timetable, a community process, and an operational system.

Format
Vertical print, approximately 1:3 ratio
Material
Large-format bond or matte paper
Source
One selected NYC street + field research + scenario studies
PROPOSAL FOR MATERIAL GESTURE

ONE BLOCK, MANY STREETS

Reconfigurable physical street kit

PLAYSEATTREELOADACCESS

A tabletop street base will be accompanied by modular pieces representing play, seating, greenery, market space, bicycle access, loading, barriers, and an emergency/local-access corridor.

Reviewers can physically rearrange the same limited street area under different priorities. The gesture materializes the project’s central trade-off: every added use occupies space, creates value, and introduces new operating responsibilities.

Format
Small tabletop model beside the printed drawing
Material
Foam board, card, wood, magnets, or laser-cut acrylic
Interaction
Move, remove, and compare modular street elements
